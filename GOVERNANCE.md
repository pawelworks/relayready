## Decision making

Maintainers decide by lazy consensus. A proposal is accepted when it has been public for the stated review period, no maintainer has raised an unresolved objection, and a maintainer records the decision. Urgent security work may be merged before public discussion when disclosure would create risk; the rationale must be published when it is safe to do so.

With one maintainer, this means Pavel Mihai Lucian makes and records final
project decisions after review; it is not multi-party consensus. Non-normative
proposals normally remain open for at least seven calendar days. A maintainer
must disclose a relevant conflict of interest. If no independent reviewer is
available, that limitation must be recorded and external review sought rather
than represented as independent approval.

## Becoming a maintainer

Contributors may be invited to become maintainers after sustained, constructive work across code, specification, review, or community support. Existing maintainers evaluate technical judgment, reliability, inclusive conduct, and the ability to act for the project rather than an employer. The decision and scope are recorded publicly.

## Specification changes

A normative specification change requires:

1. A public proposal issue describing the problem, compatibility impact, and alternatives.
2. A comment period of at least two weeks.
3. Resolution of maintainer objections under lazy consensus.
4. A specification version bump when the accepted change alters conformance.

After M1 acceptance, fixed section names and readback fields follow this process and are not changed unilaterally.
